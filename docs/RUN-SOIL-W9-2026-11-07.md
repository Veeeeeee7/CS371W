## Terms used here, in CS terms

- **SSURGO, map unit, component.** SSURGO is the USDA county soil survey, a polygon map. Each polygon belongs
  to one *map unit* (key `mukey`), a mapped soil type; think of the map as a lookup from a location to a
  `mukey`. A map unit is a mix of *components* (soil kinds), each a percentage of the unit (`comppct_r`); the
  *dominant component* is the largest. A *survey area* (`areasymbol`, about one county) is surveyed and
  versioned as a unit; `saverest` is the date of its current version.
- **Soil Data Access (SDA).** The NRCS web service that answers SQL queries against the live SSURGO database. A
  shared government server: at most 2 requests at a time, retries with backoff, every answer cached.
- **Flooding frequency class (`soil_floodfreq`).** The survey's estimate of how often a map unit floods, by
  dominant condition: None 0, Very rare 1, Rare 2, Occasional 3, Frequent 4, Very frequent 5. "None" is a class
  (no flooding), not a missing value. *Flood-rated* below means Rare or above (code ≥ 2).
- **Fluvial soil (`soil_fluvclass`).** A soil formed from river deposits (subgroups such as "Typic
  Fluvaquents"); Woznicki uses it as a sign of past flooding. Coded 1 when the dominant component's subgroup
  contains "fluv", 0 when not, 2 when the map unit is open water (Woznicki's three classes).
- **w7, w9.** The 7-feature set of the paper (`data/processed_w7`) and the 9-feature set of this experiment
  (`data/processed_w9`: the same 80,000 points and columns plus the two soil columns).
- **Cell, population, grid, degenerate, duplicate.** As in `docs/RUN-ZOO-GRIDS-2026-11-06.md`: one cell is one
  train/test split (seed 0) and the models fitted on it, one model per setting of fixed hyperparameter grids
  (369 settings); a degenerate model predicts flood for fewer than 5% or more than 95% of the ID test points and
  is dropped; duplicates are kept.
- **Tile, Zone A, Zone AE, ID, OOD.** 50 km squares; whole tiles go to training or testing. Zone AE is FEMA's
  detailed study (the models train on it); Zone A the approximate one (the out-of-distribution test set). ID =
  held-out Zone AE points in the same 28 test tiles.
- **kNN graph, piece, region, purity.** Each Zone A test point is linked to its 10 nearest Zone A test points
  (straight-line distance in metres). A *piece* is a connected component of the selected points in that graph; a
  *region* is a piece of at least 20 points. *Purity* is the share of a selected point's 10 neighbours that are
  also selected, averaged over the selected points (1 for one solid blob, S/m for random points).
- **Caliper matching, η.** A nearest-neighbour lookup: each region point looks for held-out Zone AE points with
  the same land cover whose standardized numeric inputs lie within a Euclidean distance ε (the caliper), at most
  the 5 closest, never from a test tile the region touches. *Unmatched* = nothing within ε (covariate shift:
  unusual inputs). η = the share of a matched point's matches whose label differs from its own, averaged
  (concept shift: the same inputs labelled differently). On w9 the lookup uses the 8 numeric inputs (the 6 of w7
  plus the two soil codes); land cover stays the only exact-match key.
- **Permutation importance.** The drop in a fitted model's accuracy when one feature's column is shuffled
  across the evaluation points (no refit), averaged over 5 shuffles. Rank 1 = the largest drop.
- **Datum (NAD83, WGS84).** Two slightly different models of the Earth's shape; the same longitude and latitude
  differ by about 1 m between them in Florida.

### 5.6 E6: the verdict

On the w9 Laplacian S = 500 selection:
- corr_te = **−0.935 [−0.959, −0.898]**: at or below −0.3, interval below 0;
- C6b **holds**: pooled η 0.425 ≥ 0.285 + 0.10.

So, by the rules fixed in the brief, **the result survives** (not "explained by inputs", not "vanishes"). The per-tile
accuracy gap (C1) **grew**, from +0.046 [+0.021, +0.074] to +0.066 [+0.031, +0.102]; the intervals overlap.

What the verdict does not say, and what I would weigh before adopting w9:
1. **C6a fails** (pooled unmatched 0.239), entirely because of Collier, which is covariate shift of an easy kind: no
   model fails there.
2. **C6b passes by 0.040**, and fails under the 99th-percentile caliper. The margin is thin because two of the six
   regions are easy blobs; without them η is 0.574.
3. **The regions changed.** Three of eight w7 regions are found again; Levy, the paper's first case study, is gone
   because every soil-aware model now fails it, not because it became easy.
4. **The strongest evidence on the brief's question is E2**: with the soil codes in the matching, the w7 regions' η
   stays at 0.645, and their positives lie on soils rated as never flooding. Part of the shift is not "Zone A followed
   soil maps and our features could not see them".
