# Decision provenance

Every coding decision in this repository that changes data, numbers or conclusions, and what
supports it. It was written for the code review of 2026-10-21 (docs/CODE-REVIEW-2026-10-21.md);
the `file:line` references point at the code as it stands after that review.

## How to read the tables

Each module has a table with five columns: the decision, its value, the kind of support, the
source of that support, and where it lives in the code. The kinds of support are:

| Support | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Literature | A published work states or tests it. Source gives the bib key (from `CS371_Cho_Li/custom.bib`, or a PDF under `refs/`) and the page. |
| Method source | Inherited from the method being reproduced: the OODSelect authors' released code, or the predictor list of Woznicki et al. (2019). |
| Your decision | Recorded as your (or the team's) decision in `docs/` or the paper's change logs. Source says where, and why the choice is sound or necessary. |
| Forced | Dictated by the data, a tool or an API (for example, a raster's native resolution). |
| Convention | A widely used default, with the reason it fits here. |
| None found | No published support was found. The row gives a recommendation: document it as a choice, cite something, or change it. |

"(changed in this review)" marks a decision that a bug fix or an adopted recommendation of the
review changed; the row describes the new behaviour. Where the paper describes a decision
differently from the code, the row says so and the review report gives the corrected wording.

"(archived)" marks a decision record in a superseded doc that the review moved out of `docs/`: it is in
git history and, locally, in `Claude outputs/removed-2026-10-21/docs/`.

## Held-out boundary lines and descriptive extras


These five scripts collect data the models never see: FEMA side layers, county and map-sheet lines, soil lines, rainfall descriptors and per-point provenance. ## The model population


A *cell* is one Florida run for one seed. 300 models train on Zone AE (flood zones from FEMA's detailed engineering study), and each is scored on held-out Zone AE points (in-distribution, ID) and on Zone A points (flood zones from the approximate study, out-of-distribution, OOD) taken from the same held-out tiles. A *tile* is one square of a fixed 50 km grid laid over the state. The files are listed in run order: `zoo.py` builds a cell, `audit_cell.py` checks it, and `cells.py` loads it for every later stage.

- Capacity grids (`aotil/zoo.py:108`): list the grids in an appendix, justify them by each family's ID-accuracy range, and say in the paper that each family cycles through its grid.
- Feature fractions (`aotil/zoo.py:155`): state the actual counts (3, 4 or 7 of 7 features) and justify them by the accuracy spread. Cite a random-subspace source (Ho 1998) only after reading it.
- Row fractions {0.15, 0.5, 1.0} (`aotil/zoo.py:156`): the literature supports varying training-set size, but not these values. Justify them by the spread they produce.
- Global warning filter (`aotil/zoo.py:51`): ignore only `ConvergenceWarning`.

## OODSelect and the spatial penalty


These files pick the inverted subsets. `run_oodselect_local.sh` drives a run. `oodselect_paper.py` runs one OODSelect search per cell, budget and variant. `graph.py` builds the neighbour graph that the spatial penalty and the coherence numbers use. `report_paper.py` summarises the runs against baselines. `audit_selection.py` re-derives every stored number and adds the smoothed-classic ablation.

Terms used below:
- **OOD points, d:** the Zone A points in a cell's held-out test tiles (a tile is one square of the fixed 50 km grid). d is their count, 12,771 to 15,036 in the W7 cells.
- **Budget S:** the number of OOD points a search selects. **theta** holds one learned weight per OOD point. The subset is the S points with the largest theta.
- **Selection, validation and test models:** the 300 models are split three ways. The search fits on the selection models, Optuna (the hyperparameter tuner) scores trials on the validation models, and every reported r ("test r") uses the test models only.
- **Probit:** the inverse of the standard normal CDF. Following Miller et al., accuracies go through the probit before their Pearson r is taken.
- **kNN graph and Laplacian:** each OOD point is linked to its 10 nearest OOD points. The Laplacian L is a matrix built from those links. For a 0/1 selection vector z, z'Lz counts the links that leave the selection ("cut edges").
- **Purity:** of each selected point's 10 nearest neighbours, the share that is also selected, averaged over the selected points. A random subset gives about S/d.
- **EPSG:3086 / EPSG:5070:** codes for flat map projections in metres. 3086 is Florida Albers. 5070 is the equal-area projection for the contiguous US that the rest of the pipeline uses.

## Regions, lobes and the boundary statistic


These four files run after the OODSelect selection. `regions.py` cuts the selected points into regions. `lobes.py` collects the flood-map polygons around each region and samples their edges. `edge_layers.py` measures how far each edge point lies from soil and map lines. `boundary_stat.py` computes the O5 boundary statistic U, its null and its bootstrap. The paper does not report the boundary statistic (left out after O5 was not met), but the W7 and O5 routines still run it.

Terms used below:
- **NFHL:** FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer, the digital flood map. Each polygon has a zone (A, AE, ...). **SFHA** (Special Flood Hazard Area) is the 1%-annual-chance flood zone.
- **DFIRM:** one county's digital flood map. A **FIRM panel** is one printed map sheet of it.
- **Lobe:** one single-part NFHL polygon. Its outline is the flood-zone edge the boundary analysis studies.
- **Roles:** *region* = Zone A lobes of a selected region; *matched_A* = unselected Zone A lobes nearby, matched on perimeter; *AE* = Zone AE (detailed-study) lobes in the same tiles.
- **EPSG:5070** is the CONUS Albers equal-area projection in metres; **EPSG:3086** is Florida's Albers projection. **NAD83** (EPSG:4269) is the lon/lat datum of the dataset.
- **SSURGO:** USDA's detailed soil-survey polygons ("map units"). **TIGER:** Census boundary lines. **NHDPlus:** the national stream and canal network. **NLCD:** national land-cover classes (11 = open water). **HAND:** height above the nearest stream. **DEM:** elevation raster.
- **Level set (contour-like edge):** an edge along which the ground elevation stays nearly constant, as a flood limit drawn from a computed water level would.

## The aggregate shift (O1), recurrence and matched controls


These four files sit around the OODSelect run. `o1_shift.py` measures O1, the AUROC drop from held-out Zone AE to Zone A, and the O2 aggregate correlation (W7 run, step C2). `region_recurrence.py` checks whether a region found in one seed is found again in the others (W7 step C5). `match_controls.py` (S1) and `region_null.py` (S2) build the terrain-matched controls and the matched-growth null of the O5 second round (2026-10-17). The paper reports O1 and O2 and uses the recurrence count. It replaced S1 and S2 with the region twins (CS371_Cho_Li/CHANGES-2026-10-19b.md §2 item 1), so no paper number comes from the last two files.

Terms used below:
- **Seed s:** one train/test split of the 50 km tiles, with its 300-model zoo. Seed s is the paper's split s+1.
- **ID half / OOD half:** the held-out Zone AE points and the Zone A points in a seed's test tiles (the 50 km squares held out of training).
- **AUROC (AUC):** the chance that a model scores a random flood-labeled point above a random dry-labeled one. Ties count half.
- **Cluster (tile) bootstrap:** resampling whole tiles with replacement, so points that share a tile stay together.
- **Selection / region:** the S = 500 Zone A points with the largest OODSelect weight θ. A region is a connected piece, of at least 20 points, of the spatial selection (penalty μ = 3 on a 10-nearest-neighbour graph).
- **NLCD class 11:** open water in the National Land Cover Database. **HAND:** height above the nearest drainage.
- **EPSG:4269 (NAD83)** is the dataset's lon/lat datum. **EPSG:5070** is CONUS Albers equal-area, in metres. **EPSG:3086** is Florida Albers, in metres.
- **Caliper:** the largest distance allowed between a point and its control. **SMD:** standardized mean difference, the usual balance measure in matching.
- **Stop:** a condition fixed in a task brief at which the run halts for Victor's decision.

## The O5 analyses


These seven scripts are the second O5 round: the brief is `docs/TASK-O5-REGIONS.md` (2026-10-17), the primaries were fixed in `results/2026-10-17/o5/PRIMARIES.md` before any computation, and the results are in `docs/O5-REGIONS-REPORT-2026-10-17.md`. Each part asks one question about the regions the spatial OODSelect search found. Part A asks whether the inversion is memorised label noise. Part B asks what differs beyond terrain, and Part C whether the failure is covariate or concept shift. Part D asks which input feature carries the inversion, Part E whether the region polygons were drawn differently, and Part F what adding the region labels to training costs. The paper uses only Part A (its "Memorized noise" paragraph). It dropped the terrain-matched controls, the matched-growth null, label conflict, leave-one-feature-out and the pooling experiment (`CS371_Cho_Li/CHANGES-2026-10-19b.md` §2 items 1-3), so Parts B to F feed only the O5 report. Parts B, C, E and F build on the shared pieces S1 and S2, whose own decisions are listed with `aotil/match_controls.py` and `aotil/region_null.py`.

Terms used below:
- **Seed s:** one random train/test split of the 50 km tiles (square blocks of land), with its own population of 300 models trained on Zone AE. **ID half / OOD half:** the held-out Zone AE points and the Zone A points in that seed's test tiles.
- **r (`hard_corr`):** the OODSelect statistic, the Pearson correlation across models between probit accuracy on held-out Zone AE and probit accuracy on a set of Zone A points. Probit is the inverse normal CDF. r < 0 means stronger models do worse on that set (the inversion).
- **Selection / region:** the S = 500 Zone A test points with the largest OODSelect weight θ. A region is a connected piece of at least 20 points of the spatial selection (penalty μ = 3 on a 10-nearest-neighbour graph). A region is positive-dominated if at least half of its land points carry the flood label, otherwise negative-dominated.
- **Test models (test60):** the 60 of the 300 models that the search never used. The other 180 drove the search and 60 tuned it, so a statistic that includes them is partly in-sample ("circular").
- **Interpolating model:** one that reaches at least 99% accuracy on its own training rows.
- **S1 controls:** for each region land point, an unselected Zone A test point in the same 50 km tile, with the same label and the nearest terrain (`aotil/match_controls.py`). The docstrings and logs of `o5_contrast.py` call them "terrain twins". They are not the paper's twins, which are held-out Zone AE points matched on all seven inputs.
- **S2 null patches:** 200 random connected patches of unselected Zone A points per region, grown on the 10-NN graph to the region's size inside its tiles, each with its own S1 controls (`aotil/region_null.py`). A **null percentile** is where the region's value falls among the patches' values.
- **SMD:** standardized mean difference, the difference of two means divided by a pooled SD. **TVD:** total variation distance, half the sum of the absolute differences between two sets of class shares.
- **NLCD class 11:** open water in the National Land Cover Database. **HAND** (height above nearest drainage) and **wetindexcat** (a catchment's mean wetness index) are missing on open water.
- **Cross-fitted propensity:** a classifier's probability that a point belongs to one group rather than another, where each point is scored by a model fitted on the other folds.
- **Covariate shift / concept shift:** the inputs change / the rule from inputs to labels changes.
- **Lobe:** one single-part flood-zone polygon. **Vertex spacing:** perimeter divided by the number of vertices. **FIRM panel:** one sheet of a county's flood map; **DFIRM:** a county's digital flood map. **LOMR:** a Letter of Map Revision, a later official change to a map. **STUDY_TYP / SOURCE_CIT:** NFHL fields that name a polygon's study type and source document.
- **EPSG:5070:** CONUS Albers, an equal-area map projection in metres.

#### Choices without published support

- **Panel scale as a number** (`aotil/o5_contrast.py:72`): use log2(scale) or the class-share rule, and state the choice in the O5 report.
- **5% class-share threshold** (`aotil/o5_contrast.py:46`): state it and the 20-pair condition in the report, with the findings at 2% and 10%.
- **Two-sided TVD** (`aotil/o5_contrast.py:433`): count only the upper tail (or use a permutation chi-square), and call it the variable-family test.
- **Per-region pair bootstrap in Part B** (`aotil/o5_contrast.py:776`): resample spatial blocks inside the region (`roberts2017`) or keep only the null percentile, as Part C does.
- **Numeric land-cover code in the C1-C3 space** (`aotil/o5_shift_decomp.py:309`): one-hot or exactly match land cover, and report conflict_k25 under that space.
- **C3 bootstrap without refitting** (`aotil/o5_shift_decomp.py:620`): compare with a full-refit, re-clipped bootstrap on about 100 draws, and say the interval is conditional on the fitted models.
- **Dropping clamped models in D1** (`aotil/o5_feature_inclusion.py:339`): use a continuity-corrected response or a binomial-probit fit that keeps every model.
- **Discarding rank-deficient D1 draws** (`aotil/o5_feature_inclusion.py:179`): resample within family, or print n_boot_used beside each interval.
- **Per-region lobe bootstrap in Part E** (`aotil/o5_polygons.py:1020`): block-resample by source polygon or DFIRM, or drop the per-region intervals.

- **Part A model sets** (`aotil/o5_interpolation.py:44`): the paper's −0.82 [−0.84, −0.79] is the circular all_lt099 row; quote the held-out −0.81 [−0.86, −0.74] or say most of the models were used by the search.
- **Null p-values** (`aotil/o5_contrast.py:488`, `aotil/o5_polygons.py:846`): report (1 + count as extreme)/(n + 1) instead of a percentile of 0 or 100.

## The twins analysis behind the paper's region figures


These scripts turn the stored 2026-10-16 outputs (model scores, OODSelect selections, regions) into the region-twins numbers and Figures 2 to 4 of the paper. Every file lives in `results/2026-10-19/paper_figures/`, so the Where column gives the file name and line there.

Terms used below:
- **ID test points:** held-out Zone AE points in the split's test tiles. Test tiles are the 50 km squares held out of training.
- **OOD points:** Zone A points in the same tiles.
- **Region:** a connected piece of the spatial OODSelect selection.
- **Twins:** a region's twins are ID test points with the same land cover and the most similar other inputs.
- **Reference patch:** a random connected set of ordinary points the same size as a region. Each patch gets its own twins, which shows what an unremarkable place scores.

#### Choices without published support

- **Figure 4 axes and marker sizes** (`fig_twins.py`): recheck the figure's look in the paper now that the axes span every patch.
- **Twin matching with no caliper and a mean absolute-difference cost:** add a caliper sensitivity (for example, drop pairs above 0.5 SD) and a Euclidean or Mahalanobis variant, so the "same inputs" claim does not rest on one metric.
- **5 km twin exclusion:** report d and g at 2 km and 10 km, or tie the radius to an estimated autocorrelation range of the labels.
- **15 strongest models:** rank the models on a disjoint half of the ID test set, or report g for n_strong in {10, 15, 30}.
- **0.5 cut between flood- and dry-labeled regions:** as a sensitivity, report the kind table with Collier in split 3 (mean label 0.459) moved to the other kind or excluded.
- **Point-weighted pooling:** add a version that keeps one entry per place, dropping or merging the split-3 duplicates of Sarasota and Collier.
- **Bootstrap over regions:** cluster the resampling by place, so recurring regions are not counted as independent. Give each pooled call its own seeded generator, so the intervals do not depend on call order.
- **Recurrence check:** filter to land points (NLCD ≠ 11) to match the twins, and state the 80% presence rule in the text or a footnote.

Pages are PDF pages, with the printed page number in brackets where it differs.

Terms used below:
- **SFHA (Special Flood Hazard Area):** FEMA's 1%-annual-chance ("100-year") floodplain. On the map it is split into zones. **Zone A** comes from an approximate study and has no base flood elevation. **Zone AE** comes from a detailed study and has one.
- **BFE (base flood elevation):** the computed height of the water surface in the 1% flood.
- **FIRM panel:** one sheet of a Flood Insurance Rate Map. **DFIRM_ID** identifies the study a polygon came from. "Inside FEMA's mapped area" means inside some panel.
- **Band negative:** a dry-labelled point that lies 500 m to 5 km from every SFHA.
- **Lobe:** one single-part flood-zone polygon. The boundary analysis tests whether lobe edges follow the terrain or follow held-out lines: **class S** is the soil-survey lines and **class M** is county lines and FIRM panel edges.
- **DEM:** a raster of ground elevation. **HAND (height above nearest drainage):** a point's height above the stream cell it drains to. **TWI / CTI:** the topographic wetness index ln(a / tan b), where a is the upslope drainage area and b is the slope.
- **EPSG codes:** identifiers of coordinate systems. EPSG:4269 is NAD83 longitude/latitude in degrees. EPSG:5070 is the equal-area Albers projection for the contiguous US, in metres, used for every distance. EPSG:3086 is the Florida Albers projection that the neighbour graph uses.

**Where a cited work argues against the current code.** These are code choices, not citation errors; each is recorded in its module's table above.
- `roberts2017` (p. 1): per-region intervals that resample lobes (`o5_polygons.py`) or matched pairs (`o5_contrast.py`) as if they were independent. A block bootstrap, or no per-region interval, is recommended.
- `valavi2019blockcv` (p. 4-5): `o5_shift_decomp.py` keeps the NLCD class code as a number inside a Euclidean kNN and propensity space. Categorical covariates do not belong in a distance space.
- FEMA Domain Tables (2015 p. 54, 2024 p. 67-68): the study-regime grouping in `sample_points.py` (above).
- `woznicki2019`, `samela2017`, `wing2017`: band negatives on open water stay dry-labelled (`sample_points.py`), while all three treat open water as floodplain or no-data. This is a recorded decision (docs/DATA-AUDIT-2026-10-14.md, decision 2), handled by land-only reporting.
- `kulldorff1997` (p. 13-15): its covariate-adjusted baselines are a precedent for a label-matched null, which `region_null.py` does not build. A label-weighted null exists as a sensitivity.
- `miller2021line` (p. 4): `o1_shift.py` fits its AUC slope on the raw scale. It is descriptive only.

Settled by this review: the bootstrap counts that Carpenter and Bithell 2000 argued against (now raised), the NHDPlus −9998 code (now missing), Zone D and "area not included" (now excluded from band negatives on rebuild) and open water in the Part F evaluation (now land only).

Some decisions also cite web pages that are neither in the literature index nor a PDF, so they are not listed here: 44 CFR 59.1 (the regulatory floodway definition, `lobes.py`), shapely's `orient_polygons` page (`lobes.py` inward normals), and the scikit-learn and XGBoost parameter pages and the Earth Engine catalog (see the next section).
